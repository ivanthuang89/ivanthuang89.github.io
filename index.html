The ROAS rotation problem

Network returns were declining — but not for the reason most assumed. The root cause was adverse publisher rotation.

Root cause analysis
1

Surface finding

Newsletter funded ROAS declined from 192% in 2025 to 134% in 2026. Looked like a platform problem.

2

Actual root cause

Adverse publisher rotation: churning Publisher A at $428K spend and 200% ROAS while scaling Publisher B 8× into 26% ROAS. The mix shift, not platform performance, was driving the decline.

3

Secondary driver

Deal mix shift. Reg A+ shrank from 36% to 15% of newsletter spend while Reg CF grew. Reg A+ converts at 255% ROAS vs Reg CF at 116% — the category mix was dragging the blended number.

4

Reallocation opportunity

~$1.5M in incremental funded capital FYTD recoverable by correcting the rotation. Identified before the quarter closed.

Performance Model

Designed a managed ad allocation product with pre-funded issuer treasury accounts. Piloted it, managed a $580K operational incident when it failed, paused it formally, then designed and ran a controlled three-account relaunch — all while running a 25-person media org.

What it was

A commercial product concept: pre-fund each issuer's media spend into a dedicated treasury account, then allocate from that account in real time against campaign performance. The goal was to eliminate the gap between budget approval and spend execution.

2
Treasury accounts per issuer
Each issuer maintained two accounts — one for committed media spend, one for performance-based reallocation.
$580K
Operational incident — resolved
Funds wired to wrong account during the pilot. Identified, escalated to CFO, and resolved without client impact. Led to a formal pause and process redesign.
Q3
Controlled relaunch
After the pause, ran a three-account controlled relaunch — one per Strategy Director pod — with tighter controls and clear escalation paths.

How it unfolded

From concept to pilot to incident to relaunch — a full commercial product lifecycle owned by one person.

1

Concept and design

Identified the gap between budget approval and spend execution as a source of campaign delay and performance drag. Designed the two-account treasury structure to close it. Wrote the product spec and commercial framework.

2

Pilot launch

Launched with the flagship account. The model worked operationally — real-time allocation against live campaign data, funds moving faster than the prior approval cycle.

3

$580K incident

Funds wired to the wrong account during a time-sensitive close. Identified it before client impact. Navigated the CFO escalation, sized the wire correctly, and resolved the AR exposure. Formally paused the product for Q2.

4

Post-mortem and redesign

Ran the incident post-mortem. Identified the control gap in the wire approval process. Redesigned the escalation path and added a dual-approval step before any treasury movement.

5

Controlled Q3 relaunch

Relaunched at three accounts — one per Strategy Director pod. Staged rollout, clearer controls, defined escalation path to CFO. The model ran without incident.

Why this matters

Most media leaders manage budget. This was designing a financial product that sat on top of the media infrastructure.

The commercial insight

The gap between when a client approves a budget and when that money is actually deployed costs campaign performance. Every day of delay is an impression not bought, a placement not locked, a competitor filling that inventory.

Pre-funding the treasury account removes that gap. The media team can move at market speed, not approval speed. The client gets better performance. DealMaker gets lower churn risk on its highest-spending accounts.

What the incident taught

A product that handles real money requires controls the same way a product that handles real data requires testing. The incident happened because the wire approval process had a single point of failure — one person, no dual-check.

The fix was structural, not procedural. Added a dual-approval step, defined the escalation path explicitly, and separated the person authorizing the wire from the person confirming the account. Standard fintech controls applied to a media product.

AI Workflows

Most leaders adopt AI as a personal productivity tool. I operationalized it across a 25-person org — building production-grade skills, automations, and reporting infrastructure that run weekly without my involvement.

What was built

Four deployed skills, a skills hub, a daily automation, and a weekly adoption report — all in production, all actively used.

Meta LP
Audit
Pre-launch compliance skill
Built for the sales and creative team. Claude reviews a Meta landing page against DealMaker's compliance checklist before any campaign goes live. Catches issues that previously only surfaced after launch — after spend had already been wasted.
Delegation
Brief
Work distribution skill
Structured prompt that takes a deliverable and generates a brief each recipient can upload into their own Claude session and execute independently. Used for documentation sprints, knowledge base builds, and any project requiring contributions from people who weren't in the original meetings.
Daily
Standup
Slack canvas automation
Automated daily standup that writes directly to a Slack canvas used as a live task tracker. The canvas updates without anyone touching it — open it, see where things stand. Built to replace the weekly status meeting that produced the same information a day late.
Intake
Triage
Inbound request management skill
Built for orgs where high-frequency, mixed-priority requests arrive without routing logic. The skill categorizes incoming asks into three lanes (immediate, scheduled, delegated) and surfaces the right next action without requiring a meeting to triage it.
Skills
Hub
Internal deployment infrastructure
An HTML page deployed to Cloudflare Pages that surfaces all four skills in one place — instructions, trigger phrases, and usage examples. Built so any team member can find and use the right skill without asking Ivan what prompts to run. The distribution layer for everything above.
Adoption
Report
Weekly product usage reporting
Automated weekly report posted to a Slack channel tracking adoption of CPT, CIP, and Morpheus across the org via Userpilot. The report that surfaced the Morpheus #1-of-15 stat and the CIP crossover moment — both of which became executive talking points.

The pattern

Every workflow followed the same logic: identify where the team was spending time on something that shouldn't require a person, build the minimum viable automation, deploy it, and measure adoption.

How each workflow was built
1

Identify the friction

Not "where can we use AI" but "where is the team doing something repetitive that produces a known output." Pre-launch reviews, status updates, request routing — all had predictable outputs.

2

Write the skill in Claude

Each skill is a Claude prompt with a trigger phrase, a structured output format, and a scope definition that tells it exactly what to include and exclude. No code, no API calls — just precise instruction sets.

3

Deploy via skills hub

Skills land in the hub so anyone can find them. The hub is a single HTML page on Cloudflare — no login, no onboarding. Open it, find the skill, run it.

4

Measure and report

The adoption report tracked weekly usage across all tools. That data became the evidence used to justify continued investment in internal tooling — and was cited directly in ELT conversations.
